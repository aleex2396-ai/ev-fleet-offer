Abstract—An aggregator can sell the charging flexibility of the cars parked at a workplace only if it delivers what it offers. This note builds the inner-approximation offer of Al Taha, Vincent and Bitar from per-car sets whose deadlines and energy ceilings are lower quantiles forecast at 10:00, sets one quantile level per day from past days to target delivery on 90% of days, and scores the offer by replay on the Caltech and JPL sessions of ACN-Data, with the hypotheses fixed before the test months were opened. With perfect knowledge the offer reaches ${dev_pk_of_band} of the exact band width; made from forecasts and calibrated on delivery, the population model's offer keeps about ${dev_rule_5d_share0} of that, delivered on ${dev_rule_5d_delivered} of days. A short proposition shows why the registered offers are zero: at the small shared levels the registered rules reach, a car's energy bound is zero whenever its chance of taking nothing more is at least that level, and ${empty_share} of car-days take nothing more. Knowing those cars in advance does not restore the offer; a split of each profile among the cars chosen with hindsight keeps ${dev_hind_min} to ${dev_hind_max} of it at 90% delivery. A learned per-car model keeps ${ev_h4_learned} h of each car's remaining stay against ${ev_h4_population} h for a population rule at matched coverage, and the gain does not reach the fleet offer: ${ev_h1_sec_a} against ${ev_h1_sec_b} kWh, delivered on ${ev_h1_sec_da} and ${ev_h1_sec_db} of days.

Index Terms—Electric vehicles, aggregate flexibility, inner approximation, quantile regression, conformal calibration.

I. Introduction

An aggregator that controls the charging of the cars parked at a workplace can offer the grid a set of charging profiles for the rest of the day, and is paid only if it then follows whichever profile is called. The set it can promise depends on the cars plugged in: when each will leave and how much energy each will still take. Both are unknown when the offer is made.

With each car's flexibility known, the fleet's flexibility is the Minkowski sum of the cars' sets. Al Taha, Vincent and Bitar [1] place a shifted, scaled copy of a base set inside that sum by a linear programme whose solution also allocates any profile in the copy to the cars by an affine map; for charging-only cars with one connection interval the exact sum is a generalised polymatroid [2]. Both start from sets that are known, and [6] certifies an aggregate set for cars drawn independently from one known distribution. At the time of the offer the sets are not known, and the question this note asks is what is left of the offer when each car's set is built from forecasts made at that time, and the offer must be delivered on 90% of days.

The construction is one-sided: each car's set uses a deadline no later than it is forecast to leave and an energy ceiling no higher than it is forecast to take. On any day on which all these bounds hold, every learned set lies inside the car's true set, so the offer and the allocation fixed in the morning are delivered (Proposition 1). Whether they do hold is decided by one quantile level shared by every car, chosen each morning from past days by adaptive conformal inference [3]. The evaluation replays each day against the cars actually present, in time order, on the Caltech and JPL sessions of ACN-Data [4], with the analyses, thresholds and hypotheses fixed in a tagged commit before the test months were opened.

Three results are reported. An accounting that sets the offer made with perfect knowledge of every car's set beside the exact band and beside offers made from forecasts at a 90% delivery target, with the cars that take no more energy known, and with each profile split among the cars with hindsight (Section V-A). A short proposition explaining why the offer collapses at the small shared levels the registered day-level rules reach, with the counts behind it (Proposition 2, Section V-B). And the pre-registered comparisons of a per-car learned model with a population rule, before and across the 2020 closure of the campus (Section V-C).

II. Problem

A. Per-car sets

The offer is made at 10:00 for 10:00 to 20:00, in K = 40 steps of δ = 0.25 h. A car with deadline d (a number of steps), energy ceiling e (kWh after 10:00) and power cap c can follow any profile in

U(d,e)={u∈ℝK:0≤uk≤c1k≤d,δ∑k=1Kuk≤e}(1)

where 1k≤d is 1 up to the deadline and 0 after it: a charging-only case of the generalised battery model of [1, eq. (2)], with the energy ceiling as the upper limit on cumulative energy and no lower limit. The zero profile lies in every set, so a delivered offer means the fleet can follow the profile within caps, deadlines and ceilings, not that each driver was charged. The true set of a car uses its unplug time rounded down to the grid (20:00 if later) and the energy it took after 10:00; the cap is an assumed c = 6.7 kW.

B. The offer and its allocation

For n cars with sets Ui and a base set U0, Theorem 2 of [1] gives sufficient linear conditions for p + αU0 ⊆ ∑i Ui; programme (24) of [1] maximises α under them, and its Section IV allocates a profile p + αu0 to car i as γi + Γiu0 [1, eq. (31)], fixed when the offer is made. The base set is either fixed from the training period or the day-average base set, whose constraint vector is the average of the present cars' [1, eq. (11)]. When every car's set forces uk = 0 at a step where U0 can charge, programme (24) has no point with α > 0, and with α ≥ 0 allowed its optimum is 0 (proved in the repository), which a base set fixed in advance meets often; a one-dimensional offer, a segment b + av, |a| ≤ A, that moves charging out of 10:00–11:00 and into 11:00–13:00, was registered as the fallback for that case.

Cumulative energy by step t is linear in u, so the exact band of the fleet's cumulative energy is

[0,∑i=1nmin(ei,cδmin(t,di))](2)

and the size of an offer from programme (24) is its band width averaged over 14:00, 16:00 and 18:00, in kWh, beside the same average of (2). The size of the one-dimensional offer is its amplitude A, the energy it moves out of or into 10:00–11:00, which is not on the same scale.

C. Learned sets and containment

Proposition 1: If d′ ≤ d and e′ ≤ e, then U(d′, e′) ⊆ U(d, e).

Proof: For u in U(d′, e′), 0 ≤ uk ≤ c for k ≤ d′, uk = 0 otherwise, and δ∑uk ≤ e′ ≤ e. ∎

A learned set uses a lower quantile of the car's remaining stay, rounded down to the grid, and a lower quantile of its remaining energy. On the event E that every present car's two bounds hold, each learned set lies in its true set; Minkowski sums preserve inclusion, so the offer computed on the learned sets lies in the true sum, and the allocation fixed at 10:00 gives each car a profile in its true set. By the union bound, Pr(E) ≥ 1 − (sum of the 2n miss probabilities) with no assumption on how cars depend on each other. E is sufficient for delivery, not necessary, and the inclusion fails for a lower limit on energy, which is why there is none.

D. How a day is scored

Each day's offer is checked on nine profiles: for each of 12:00, 14:00, 16:00 and 18:00, the two that move the most energy across it, and a least-cost profile under a stylised tariff. Primary check: each car follows its allocation as far as its true set allows; the day is delivered if no profile leaves more than 10−4 kWh undelivered. Secondary check: the largest energy the cars present can take within their true sets under the profile's total power, an upper bound that needs hindsight (the hindsight split). Offers are compared only at matched delivery rates.

III. Learned bounds and the shared level

A. Per-car models

The learned model is gradient-boosted quantile regression (pinball loss, one model per level and target) of remaining stay and remaining energy, on inputs known at 10:00: time and calendar, station, cars present, the driver's earlier sessions and a typed plan if recorded in time, refitted monthly on the past 90 days. The population rule gives each car the Kaplan–Meier quantiles of the site's recent sessions given the time it has been plugged in; the stratified rule gives empirical quantiles of earlier 10:00 car-days in its cell (plug-in hour, Friday, registered driver). All three predict the same 16 levels; a level between two is interpolated.

B. One level per day

Every present car gets its two bounds at one level q. A day's score s is the largest grid level at which every bound of the day held, so all held at q exactly when q ≤ s. With m scored days in the past 90 calendar days, a rule at miss level a uses the ⌊a(m+1)⌋-th smallest score, and adaptive conformal inference [3, eq. (2)] moves a after each scored day:

at+1=at+γ(0.10−errt),a1=0.10,γ=0.03.(3)

Summing (3) gives, whatever the days do, a share of failed days over T scored days from working level aS of at most 0.10 + (aS + 0.9γ)/(γT), the side of [3, Prop. 4.1] a delivery target needs (from the level each rule had reached when the frozen evaluation began, ${ev_aci_min} to ${ev_aci_max} over its ${ev_h1_reg_days} scored weekdays), provided each outcome is known before the next decision; a car that stays overnight delays it, and the rule updates as outcomes arrive. In the registered rule errt = 1 when some bound failed. A secondary rule, added after the first development results and labelled as such, sets errt = 1 when the offer at the level used, or at a lower level, was not delivered, and uses the day-average base set. The union bound, each bound at level 0.10/(2n), is a further arm.

C. Zero ceilings at small levels

The level a day-level target needs is small: 0.10/(2n) is ${union_level} at the median of ${median_cars} cars present, and the registered conformal rules settle between ${level_learned} and ${level_population} (Section V); the rules calibrated on delivery run at higher levels. At this site the energy a car still takes after 10:00 has an atom at zero: ${empty_share} of car-days through August 2019 take nothing more. Let Ei ≥ 0 be car i's remaining energy, Xi its inputs, Fi(e) = P(Ei ≤ e | Xi), πi = Fi(0), and its bound at level q the quantile êi(q) = inf{e ≥ 0 : Fi(e) ≥ q}.

Proposition 2: êi(q) = 0 if and only if πi ≥ q.

Proof: If πi ≥ q, 0 is in the set and Ei ≥ 0, so êi(q) = 0. If πi < q, right-continuity gives Fi(ε) < q for some ε > 0, so êi(q) ≥ ε. ∎

A car with a zero ceiling adds nothing to (2), and the offer lies inside the sum of the learned sets, so at level q only cars with πi < q can contribute: at q = ${union_level} a car counts only if its inputs rule out, to within that probability, that it takes nothing more. The same holds for the deadline with the chance of leaving within 15 minutes. Proposition 1 holds at every level; it assumes the bounds hold, not that they are informative. Proposition 2 was written after the first development run showed offers near zero and before the counts below were read; for the empirical rules it holds exactly on the grid, for the trees only as far as they estimate the conditional quantile.

IV. Data and protocol

ACN-Data [4] records each charging session's plug-in and unplug times, energy and, for registered drivers, a typed request. Decisions are made at 10:00 on weekdays; a day is scored if at least five present cars take more than 0.5 kWh after 10:00. The energy a car takes after 10:00 is not recorded; a per-session time series that would give it failed a quality check fixed before it was fetched, so it is estimated by spreading the session's energy evenly from plug-in to the end of charging, and the ceiling is approximate.

Caltech sessions through August 2019 are development data (May–August 2019 for model selection); September 2019 to February 2020 is the frozen evaluation; from 1 March 2020 is the sealed test, which spans the campus closure, after which the median stay fell from about six hours to under two; its scored weekdays are ${se_days_2020} in March 2020, before the closure, and ${se_days_2021} from August 2021; the JPL site is a replication, run once with the Caltech-trained pipeline frozen and, separately, updating on JPL days. The sealed periods were looked at while the project was scoped, for stay statistics and a reading of raw sessions; the evaluation months also gave the energy of 50 sampled sessions and the daily counts of cars present and taking energy, which set the adaptive rule's step. Every look is listed in the repository, and what they touched is read as a check rather than a blind test. Hypotheses H1–H4, their thresholds and the written predictions were committed and tagged before any confirmatory run. On the model-selection months the perfect-knowledge offer's α was below 0.2 on 37.6% of scored weekdays with the fixed base set, above the registered threshold of 25%, so every registered arm uses the one-dimensional fallback.

VI. Discussion

None of the guarantees used here bounds the offer's size. Proposition 1 needs every bound to hold, not to be informative; at the levels the registered rules reach with ${median_cars} cars, most energy bounds are zero (Proposition 2). The adaptive rule's long-run bound on the share of failed days [3, Prop. 4.1], in the one-sided form used here, holds whatever the days do, provided each outcome is known before the next decision; a car that stays overnight delays it. It is met by an offer of zero. The finite-sample bound of [5, Thm. 4] for chance constraints under drift assumes independent days, a known drift bound on whole-day configurations and a finite decision set, which these data do not provide; its day count alone (${theorem_days} days at the two settings computed here) is met at the first setting by the ${days_before_change} weekdays before March 2020. No certificate from it is claimed.

Reliability was recovered by giving up size. Delivered on 90% of days, the offer made from forecasts keeps at most a few percent of the offer perfect knowledge supports, and the learned per-car model, whose stay bounds are tighter than the population's at matched coverage (H4), gives the smaller fleet offer once both are calibrated on delivery. The diagnostics are consistent with the formulation mattering more than the forecasts: a shared level makes every car's bound as cautious as the fleet's target requires, and under the allocation fixed at 10:00 a car's miss can become the fleet's. A per-car level and an online re-split were not tested.

The limits of the evidence are as follows. The energy a car takes after 10:00 is estimated, not measured, and the site's own scheduler shapes it: cars took a median 0.64 of what their drivers asked for. Whether a car is still charging at 10:00, which an operator would see, is not recorded, so the diagnostic in which the empty cars are known stands in for it. The power cap of 6.7 kW is assumed for every station, with 3.3 and 11 kW run as sensitivities. Only nine profiles of each offer are checked. H3 is untested for lack of days after the reopening, and the sealed months held ${se_h1_sec_days} scored weekdays, so their comparisons are wide. The evaluation months and both sealed periods were looked at while the project was scoped (Section IV); the list is in the repository.
