# Analysis plan: a fleet charging offer from learned per-car bounds, calibrated by day and scored by replayed delivery

Draft 2, 5 November 2026. This document is the plan of the public project `ev-fleet-offer`. It is written before any confirmatory run and before any sealed data are opened. It will be committed to `docs/` and frozen by the git tag `analysis-plan`; "pre-registered" below means fixed in this document before that tag. The loader opens the sealed data only once the tag exists. Everything in it is a plan, not a result.

## 1. The question

An aggregator that controls the charging of many electric vehicles (EVs) can sell flexibility to the grid only if it can say in advance how much charging it can shift, and deliver what it said. At a workplace or campus car park the offer depends on the cars plugged in: when each will leave and how much energy each will still take. Both are uncertain, and both change over time with seasons, prices, who comes, and shocks.

The offer can be built in two ways. A **population offer** gives every present car bounds from the same rule, taken from recent sessions at the site and conditioned only on how long the car has already been plugged in. A **learned offer** gives each car its own bounds, predicted from what is known about that car and its driver at the moment the offer is made. The learned offer uses individual information in addition to the site's recent history.

**Headline question.** At a fixed morning decision time, how much flexible charging can a site offer for the rest of the day, deliverable on at least 90% of days, when each present car's departure and energy are learned bounds rather than site-wide bounds; how much of the offer does the learned model add over an equally updated population offer; and how does the answer change across a real change in who uses the site and how?

Three outcomes are possible, and each is worth reporting:

- the learned offer is larger at the same delivery rate and keeps its margin across the change;
- once both offers are calibrated at the level of whole days, the learned offer is no larger than the population offer, so per-car accuracy does not survive aggregation;
- offers calibrated car by car fail at fleet level right after the change, on days when many drivers' errors share a sign, and restoring 90% of days costs a measured share of the offer.

These are the outcomes the design can distinguish. A failed data check or a degenerate offer is a fourth outcome and is reported as such (Sections 10 and 10a).

The project also reports a per-car result on the way, because the fleet offer is built from it: how many more hours a 90%-reliable "this car stays at least X more hours" bound keeps when it uses what is known about the car and driver than when it uses past stays alone, before and across the change.

## 2. What is not known, and what the theory leaves unsupported

**What is uncertain.** The aggregate-flexibility method of Al Taha, Vincent and Bitar (2024) takes each car's set as given. With learned bounds, the offered set is guaranteed to lie inside the fleet's true set when every present car's bounds hold on that day, and may or may not otherwise. A model can be well calibrated car by car (each car's bound holds about 90% of the time) while the fleet offer is not, in either direction: if cars' errors are independent, failed bounds may offset under reallocation and the offer is delivered more often than 90%, wasting flexibility; if errors are shared within a day (a holiday, a new fee, a reopening), failures cluster on exactly those days. Which happens, and how often, is measured, not assumed.

**A possible mechanism.** A behaviour change can shift many drivers at once, turning independent errors into shared ones. The 2024 paper takes each car's set as given, and the robust aggregation result of Mukhi, Qu, You and Abate (2025) is a guarantee for a fleet whose cars are drawn independently from one known distribution; neither describes errors shared within a day or a distribution that changes. This is a hypothesis to test against other explanations: a changed fleet composition, the energy proxy, and the base set's fit to the day.

**What it costs.** The project measures how much flexibility must be given up to restore 90% of days after the change, for each offer, in kilowatt-hours and as a share of what perfect knowledge would allow.

**What the theory leaves unsupported.** The fleet-level chance constraint has one sample per day. The finite-sample guarantee of Yan, Parise and Bitar (2022) for chance constraints under drift, applied with days as samples, needs independent days, a known bound on how far the distribution of whole-day configurations can drift, and 312 or 1,121 past days at the two settings of Section 6. The development record holds 483 weekdays, enough at the first setting and not at the second; a rolling year holds about 250 and 2021 holds 182, too few at both. More binding than the count, the drift bound would have to cover the 2020 change, which no earlier data can measure, and the offer is a continuous, data-dependent decision rather than a fixed finite set of 101 scales. So the day-level rate in this project rests on a calibration rule over past days or on a union bound across cars, and the project reports the theorem's requirements beside the days available.

**A null result.** If the learned offer adds nothing over the population offer at the delivery target, the project reports no measured benefit from per-car information under these models, data and service assumptions. Comparisons of raw prediction quality, the stratified population arm (Section 5), the oracle gap and the base set's fit distinguish the possible explanations; a null alone does not say which binds. That answers a question aggregators face, whether to invest in models of individual drivers, and it gives a measured comparison, on public data in time order, of what predicting individual drivers adds inside an aggregation method. Thrän, Mareček, Shorten and Green (2025) write that "Aggregation avoids the difficult prediction of individual driving behaviour" and forecast fleet boundaries directly; this project does not implement that route and does not test it.

## 3. The decision being modelled

- **Decision time:** 10:00 Pacific time on each weekday. The offer covers the cars plugged in at 10:00 (plugged in before 10:00 and not unplugged by then), so every input is known at the time of the decision and no forecast of later arrivals is needed. The choice of hour is checked on development data (Section 10a, D3); 09:00 is a pre-registered sensitivity. Days with fewer than five present cars with energy remaining are not scored and are counted.
- **Horizon:** 10:00 to 20:00 in 15-minute steps (40 steps).
- **Per-car flexibility set.** For car i present at 10:00, a charging-only special case of the generalised battery model of the 2024 paper (its equation (2)), built as in its Example 1 with two changes: no discharge (minimum power 0), and an energy ceiling in place of the energy requirement of its equation (7). Charging power is between 0 and a cap while the car is plugged in and zero after its deadline, and cumulative energy delivered after 10:00 is at most the energy the car will still take. The set asks nothing of the car: the zero profile is inside every set, a profile may charge a car less than it needs, and a delivered day does not mean that drivers received their energy. The energy given to the cars under each delivered profile is reported, and a required-energy version is a sensitivity (below). The true set uses the actual unplug time (20:00 if the car is still plugged in then) and the energy actually delivered between 10:00 and 20:00, with the deadline rounded down to the 15-minute grid and the same power cap as the learned set. The learned set replaces both with one-sided bounds: a deadline no later than the car is predicted to leave (a lower quantile of remaining stay, rounded down to the grid) and an energy ceiling no higher than the car is predicted to take (a lower quantile of remaining energy). Containment (Section 6, Proposition) then holds whenever both bounds hold; this is sufficient, not necessary.
- **Power cap:** 6.7 kW per car (32 A at 208 V is 6.66 kW; the garage's 54 original stations are rated 6.7 kW) as the primary assumption, one cap per station group if the development data show stations on higher-rated lines (Section 10a, D2), with 3.3 kW and 11 kW as sensitivities. The pilot signal in the time series is the scheduler's current limit sent to the car, not a capability of the vehicle; a realised-cap sensitivity uses a session's maximum pilot only in a labelled replay sensitivity, never as a feature or as capability at 10:00. The cap is an assumption of the service model, not a measured capability of the vehicles, and is reported as such. The garage's shared supply limit is not modelled; a sensitivity caps total offered power at a stated share of the panel rating.
- **Energy.** "Energy the car will still take" is the energy the network actually delivered between 10:00 and 20:00, computed from the per-session charging-current series (Section 7). At this site that quantity is shaped partly by the network's own scheduler and, for sessions not linked to a driver, by a policy that ended unclaimed sessions after 30 minutes from 1 November 2018 (Lee, Li and Low, 2019), so it is a proxy for the driver's need. A sensitivity restricts the cohort to sessions with a typed energy request recorded before 10:00 and uses the remaining request (request minus energy delivered by 10:00, clipped at zero) instead. A second sensitivity adds a required-energy floor per car at an upper quantile of its energy need, the deadline still a lower quantile, and reports the share of cars whose learned set is then empty.
- **The offer.** The structure-preserving inner approximation of the 2024 paper (its linear programme (24)): a base set U₀ of the form of the paper's equation (11), with its vector h₀ fixed from the training period (through April 2019), committed at the tag and identical across arms, is shifted and scaled so that p + αU₀ fits inside the sum of the per-car sets, using the conditions of Theorem 2; α is maximised. The paper's own choice, h₀ as the average of the present cars' vectors on the day, is a sensitivity. The offer is the set p + αU₀. Its **size** is reported as the width of the cumulative-energy band it allows at 14:00, 16:00 and 18:00, averaged, in kWh, together with α (for a fixed base set the width is α times a constant, so the two carry the same ranking). The exact width of the fleet's cumulative-energy band at the same times, Σᵢ min(eᵢ, c·δ·min(t, dᵢ)) for the ceiling-only sets above with cap c, step δ, deadline dᵢ and ceiling eᵢ (the exact aggregate set in general is in Mukhi, Qu, You and Abate, 2025, and Mukhi, Loho and Abate, 2026), is reported beside it, so that the conservatism of the inner approximation is measured and not assumed. If the programme fails the run-time or degeneracy check of Section 10a (D9), the fallback is a one-dimensional offer: a segment b + a·v around a feasible reference profile b, with a zero-mean shift shape v (less charging 10:00–11:00, more 11:00–13:00) scaled by an amplitude a within a stated range, one explicit allocation per car, and every resulting profile non-negative and within the caps. The trigger is the number in D9, evaluated on development data only; a degenerate result on sealed data is reported, not used to switch method.
- **Delivered on a day.** A fixed finite set of profiles is drawn from the offer: the profiles that reach the most and the least cumulative energy at each of the four checkpoints 12:00, 14:00, 16:00 and 18:00 (eight profiles), and the profile that costs least under a fixed time-of-use tariff (one). For each, two checks. **Primary:** the allocation fixed at 10:00 by Section IV of the 2024 paper (uᵢ = γᵢ + Γᵢu₀, its equation (31), from the programme solved on the learned sets) is checked against each car's true set; the day counts as delivered if all nine allocations fit, and otherwise the shortfall in kWh is recorded. **Secondary, an upper bound:** a feasibility linear programme splits each profile among the cars actually present knowing their true sets, which no aggregator could do at 10:00 (this is the generic disaggregation that the paper's Section IV contrasts with its affine map). This checks nine profiles, not every profile in the offer, and the results say so. The tariff, the solver and its feasibility tolerance, and tie-breaking are fixed before the tag.

## 4. The learned model

- **What it predicts.** For each car present at 10:00: quantiles of its remaining stay in hours and of the energy it will take between 10:00 and 20:00 in kWh, on a grid of levels from 0.002 to 0.95. Levels below 0.05 are needed by the union-bound arm; where development data cannot support a level, the trivial bound is used (Section 10a, D10).
- **Inputs known at 10:00.** The session: plug-in time, elapsed time, weekday, month, academic-calendar and holiday flags from a calendar committed at the tag, station, the number of cars present at 10:00, and the price regime (for sessions linked to a driver, charging was free until 1 November 2018 and $0.12 per kWh after; unclaimed sessions have always been free). The driver, for sessions linked to a registered driver by an app entry recorded before 10:00 (a session claimed later counts as anonymous at 10:00): counts and quantiles of stay and energy from that driver's earlier sessions that had ended before 10:00, and days since the last visit. The driver's typed plan, only if recorded before 10:00, with flags for stays of exactly 60 and exactly 480 minutes, which look like app defaults (typed stays of exactly 8 hours were almost absent before March 2020 and were 26% to 36% of typed stays in 2021, `docs/prior-looks.md`) and are not called defaults without a dated source.
- **Models.** Gradient-boosted decision trees trained with pinball loss (quantile regression) as the primary family; a small neural network with one output per quantile as the second. Both are refitted at monthly origins; each refit sees only sessions that had ended before its date and excludes sessions that began within 24 hours of it (the same rule for the population arms, so right-censoring is handled alike). Settings are chosen on an internal split of the training period (through April 2019) and on the model-selection months May to August 2019, and are frozen before any later month is scored; the full grid of settings searched is printed in `docs/theory.md` at the tag.
- **Validation.** Pinball loss, coverage of each quantile, and calibration plots, separately for known and anonymous drivers. The duration and energy errors (SMAPE) of Lee, Li and Low (2019, Table 2) are also reported as context; their arrival-time forecasts of whole-session duration differ from this project's 10:00 remaining-stay target, so the values are not a direct reproduction.
- **Baselines.** The Kaplan–Meier quantile of remaining stay among recent sessions that had lasted at least as long as the car has been plugged in, and the matching quantile of remaining energy (the population rule); a stratified table by plug-in hour, weekday type and claimed status; a linear quantile regression; each driver's own past quantiles; the Gaussian-mixture predictor of Lee, Li and Low, converted to quantiles of remaining stay; the typed plan taken at face value. If the learned model does not beat each driver's own past quantiles or the stratified table, the "learned" claim reduces to that, and the results say so.

## 5. Offers compared

All arms use the same per-car set form, the same base set, the same programme and the same two delivery checks. Arms 4, 5 and 6 share the calibration rule, history window, refit schedule and target; arm 3 has its own rule; the oracle is a reference only.

1. **Oracle:** each car's true set. The ceiling within the chosen cap, set form and base set; no real aggregator reaches it. It is delivered on every day by construction, so Figures 2 and 3 mark it as the 100% line.
2. **Population:** every car gets bounds from the same rule: quantiles of remaining stay and energy from the site's recent ended sessions (a window fixed before the tag), conditional on how long the car has already been plugged in at 10:00, updated on the same schedule as the learned model.
3. **Learned, union bound:** each car's two bounds (deadline and energy) at the quantile level 0.10 divided by twice the number of present cars (0.00125 with 40 cars), so that, if each of the 2n bounds holds with at least its probability given what is known at 10:00, all hold at once with probability at least 0.90 without any assumption about independence between cars. These levels lie far below the model's working range; where a level cannot be supported on development data (Section 10a, D10) the car's bounds are set to the trivial values (deadline at 10:00, energy zero) and the share of such cars is reported. This arm is expected to be very conservative.
4. **Learned, calibrated by day:** one shared quantile level for all cars, set so that every present car's bounds would have held on at least 90% of past days. The day score is, for each car, the larger of its two standardised shortfalls (deadline and energy, standardised by scales fitted on training data only), maximised over the day's cars; each day's score is stored on that day under the model then in force and is never recomputed. Primary rule: adaptive conformal inference with days as steps, with its step γ, its starting level and its handling of levels outside [0, 1] fixed before the tag. Secondary rule: a split conformal rule on a window of past day scores, which needs the days to be exchangeable; across the change they are not, so its 90% is an empirical target. The calibrated event, every bound holds, is sufficient for delivery but not the same, so realised delivery will exceed the target and will differ between arms; achieved delivery is reported beside every size. The day's outcome is defined within the horizon (deadline and energy as of 20:00), so it is known by 20:00, before the next decision.
5. **Population, calibrated by day:** arm 2 with arm 4's calibration, so the learned-against-population comparison is at a matched target.
6. **Stratified population, calibrated by day:** quantiles of remaining stay and energy by plug-in hour, weekday type and claimed status only, with no driver history and no learned model, and the same calibration. Arm 4 against arm 6 shows what individual and learned information adds beyond the time of day; arm 4 against arm 5 is the primary comparison.

The primary outcome is the paired difference in offer size between arm 4 and arm 5 under the same frozen calibration rule targeting 90% delivery, over the weekdays of the frozen evaluation periods (Section 7), reported beside both arms' achieved delivery rates, with uncertainty from week-block resampling; monthly values are reported as a series. Offer sizes read off each arm's size-against-delivery curve at a realised 90% (Figure 3) are a secondary, retrospective summary and never select a setting. The oracle gives the value of perfect information; arm 3 gives the price of the every-car-at-once condition.

## 6. The theory the project reads against

Statements below are taken from the papers' texts, with their numbering, and are re-verified against the papers before the `analysis-plan` tag. Each paper's symbols are kept within its paragraph: α is the scaling factor of programme (24) here and in Section 3, the share of discarded samples in the 2022 theorem, and the target miss rate in Gibbs and Candès; γᵢ is a per-car translation in the 2024 paper and γ the step size in adaptive conformal inference.

**Al Taha, Vincent and Bitar (IEEE Transactions on Smart Grid, 2025, online 2024; arXiv 2207.07067).** Each car's flexibility set is a polytope Uᵢ = {u : Hu ≤ hᵢ} (the generalised battery model, equations (2)–(3)); the fleet's aggregate flexibility is the Minkowski sum Σ Uᵢ, which is intractable to compute exactly in general. Theorem 2: for a base set U₀ = {u : Hu ≤ h₀}, the set p + P U₀ lies inside Σ Uᵢ if there exist γᵢ and Γᵢ (unrestricted) and Λᵢ ≥ 0 (entrywise) with [p, P] = Σ [γᵢ, Γᵢ] (equation (20)), ΛᵢH = HΓᵢ (22) and Λᵢh₀ ≤ hᵢ − Hγᵢ (23). These conditions are sufficient and, in the paper's own word, conservative; they are linear, so the inner approximation is a linear programme (programme (24) for the structure-preserving case P = αI). Section IV allocates an aggregate profile u = p + Pu₀ to car i as uᵢ = γᵢ + Γᵢu₀ (equation (31)), an affine map obtained as a by-product, which the paper presents as the way to avoid solving a disaggregation linear programme. Example 1 (equations (4)–(7)) builds a car's set with a power range (which may include discharge), a capacity ceiling and a lower energy limit in force after the deadline, the energy the driver requires (equation (7)); the sets of Section 3 drop that requirement. The paper's base set (equation (11)) is the average of the present cars' vectors; any H-polytope base set is allowed. The paper's experiments take arrival, departure and energy as given and, in the comparison with homothets, set the same arrival and departure for every car.

**What this project adds to that statement.** *Proposition (containment under one-sided bounds).* Write U(d, e) for the set of Section 3 with charging allowed up to deadline d and cumulative energy after the decision time at most e, with the same power cap. If d′ ≤ d and e′ ≤ e then U(d′, e′) ⊆ U(d, e), because a profile that is zero after d′ is zero after d and a cumulative energy at most e′ is at most e; sums of sets preserve inclusion. Hence, on the event that every present car's learned deadline is no later than its true departure (both on the 15-minute grid, the learned one rounded down) and its learned energy ceiling is no higher than the energy it took, with the same cap in both sets, any inner approximation of the sum of learned sets lies inside the sum of true sets, and every offered profile can be delivered. By the union bound, the probability of that event is at least one minus the sum of the miss probabilities of the 2n bounds; beyond that it is an empirical quantity, measured by day. The event is sufficient for delivery, not necessary. The inclusion fails for an energy requirement (a lower limit: a smaller predicted requirement gives a larger set, so containment would need an upper quantile) and for an exact energy target (no one-sided bound gives containment). The proposition is elementary; it is stated so that the project claims what the theorem gives and no more.

**Yan, Parise and Bitar (IEEE Control Systems Letters, 2022; arXiv 2205.03748).** Samples are independent, not necessarily identically distributed, and the 1-Wasserstein distance between the distributions of samples i and i + k is at most ρ(k), a known function with ρ(0) = 0 (condition (9)). Each sample is replaced by a norm ball around it; with the radii of Remark 4, rᵢ = ρ(N + 1 − i)/θ, the radius is the drift budget at the sample's age divided by θ. Theorem 4: for a finite decision set X, the probability that the robust sampled solution set is not inside the true ε-feasible set is at most |X| · Ψ(αN; p₁, …, p_N), with pᵢ = (ε − ρ(N + 1 − i)/rᵢ)₊ and Ψ the distribution function of a sum of independent Bernoulli variables; with the radii of Remark 4, every pᵢ = ε − θ. Corollary 1 then gives the sufficient sample size N ≥ ln(|X|/δ) / (2(ε − α − θ)²). Applied with days as samples and a grid of 101 scaling levels as X (ε = 0.10, δ = 0.05), the Theorem 4 bound is at most δ for every N ≥ 312 at α = 0.02, θ = 0.04 and for every N ≥ 1,121 at α = 0.05, θ = 0.025 (it first dips below δ at 285 and 1,057); Corollary 1 asks for 2,379 and 6,089 days, about 7.6 and 5.4 times more. The development record holds 483 weekdays, so the count is met at the first setting and missed by 638 days at the second; a rolling year (about 250 weekdays) and 2021 (182 weekdays) meet neither. The count is not the main obstacle: the theorem also needs independent days, a norm on whole-day configurations (a varying number of cars, each with a deadline and an energy), a known drift bound ρ on that space (its radii are 25 to 40 times the drift budget), and a fixed finite decision set, which the continuously fitted offer is not. The project reports the requirement beside the days available and uses the theorem's own radii, ρ/θ, to show what a drift allowance would cost at day level. No certificate from this theorem is claimed.

**Gibbs and Candès (NeurIPS 2021), Proposition 4.1.** With the working level updated as αₜ₊₁ = αₜ + γ(α − errₜ), the average miss rate over T steps is within (max{α₁, 1 − α₁} + γ)/(γT) of the target, with no constraints on the data-generating distribution. With days as steps this certifies a long-run share of failed days, not a probability for each day. The result assumes the stated recursion with an initial level in [0, 1], a positive step, and a quantile rule extended to infinite endpoints; over the 182 weekdays of 2021 the bound is within 5 points of 10% only if γ ≥ 0.11. What each day-level rule gives: the split conformal rule gives at least 90% for the next day only if days are exchangeable, which the change breaks; adaptive conformal inference gives the long-run share above; the union bound gives at least 90% for each day if every per-car bound is valid at its level.

**Mukhi, Qu, You and Abate (HSCC 2025).** The robust aggregate flexibility set at confidence 1 − β is the set of aggregate profiles that the realised fleet can satisfy with probability at least 1 − β (Definition 2.3), under charging requirements drawn independently from one known distribution. Theorem 4.4 gives a generalised polymatroid contained in that set, built as the intersection of the aggregate sets of all populations within a Wasserstein ball that contains the realised population with probability at least 1 − β (its Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.2). The paper names per-vehicle distributions as a limit of its independent-draws model. The project's empirical target differs: the realised day, with the distribution unknown, changing, and sessions not independent, scored on nine profiles; it reports empirical delivery rather than that certificate and names the result as the one that would apply if its assumptions held.

## 7. Data

**Source.** Caltech ACN-Data (Lee, Li and Low, 2019): the Caltech campus car park (54 charging points) and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) workplace site, available for educational and research use after registration; not redistributed. Session fields: plug-in, charging-done and unplug times, energy delivered, station, a driver identifier where the driver used the app, and the driver's typed plan with its timestamp. Energy delivered after 10:00 and the pilot signal are not fields of the session record: they come from the charging-current and pilot-signal time series, which the API serves from its time-series endpoint, one session per request. The download, its coverage by month, and the rule for sessions without a usable series are part of `docs/data.md` and of Section 10a (D1).

**Development data: Caltech, 25 April 2018 to 29 February 2020.** 27,584 sessions and 483 calendar weekdays; the number of scored weekdays (at least five present cars with energy remaining) is counted and reported. The shares of sessions linked to a driver by 10:00 and with a typed plan recorded before 10:00 are recomputed here with their definitions (figures reported earlier, 47% and 46%, came from a prior look and are not treated as verified). This period contains a real change that is not sealed: charging became paid for claimed sessions on 1 November 2018, after which the evening community peak vanished (Lee, Li and Low, 2019). The method is trained across that change and evaluated on the months that follow it. Within the period: training through April 2019; model selection May to August 2019 (88 weekdays; settings frozen at its end; these months also warm up the day-level calibration); frozen development evaluation September 2019 to February 2020 (130 weekdays), on which H1 and H4 are scored with day-level calibration using only earlier days and on which the end-to-end pipeline is rehearsed. Nothing in the frozen evaluation chooses a setting.

**Sealed test data, opened once each after the `analysis-plan` tag.** Caltech from 1 March 2020 Pacific time (24 sessions in April 2020, none in September and October 2020; 182 weekdays from 1 January to 13 September 2021). The 2021 reopening is defined by a rule applied in time order: the first weekday of 2021 from which the running average over ten weekdays of cars present at 10:00 is at least five; H3's window starts there. Then JPL, once, in two pre-registered tracks: the Caltech-trained pipeline with no JPL updates (no refit, no new calibration scores, no new driver history), and the same pipeline updating from JPL outcomes as they mature; the first track is primary.

**What has already been seen.** Earlier looks at both sealed periods are listed in `docs/prior-looks.md`. They include stay and typed-stay statistics for every Caltech day from March 2020 to September 2021 (the fall in median stay from 6.05 to 1.68 hours between summer 2019 and summer 2021, the halving of sessions per day, 26% to 36% of typed stays at exactly 8 hours in 2021), how often fixed and rolling per-car lower bounds on stay held (37% to 87% and about 85% to 95% of sessions after the closure), a count-based fleet promise at 14:00 fitted in summer 2019 that held on no 2021 weekday, the day-to-day dispersion of early departures in summer 2021, the drift radii and sample sizes of the 2022 rule at the observed distances, and, at JPL, stay statistics for a random sample of days and for May to August 2021 with fixed and rolling bounds holding in 74% to 96% and 71% to 100% of sessions. No learned per-car bound, no offer from the inner approximation, no day-level calibration rule and no learned-against-population comparison has been computed on them. The sealed test is therefore a **frozen retrospective comparison on previously explored eras**: blind for the learned bounds, the offers and the calibrated delivery rates; not blind for the size and direction of the shift, for the failure of frozen population-style rules, for the dispersion of departures across days, or for the drift of typed plans. H3's first clause is largely predicted by these looks and is reported as a check, not a blind test. Freezing this plan prevents later adaptation to its outputs; it does not make the earlier outcomes unseen. No claim of an untouched test set is made.

**Pre-registered before the sealed loader opens:** the decision time, horizon and step; the definition of "present at 10:00" and the minimum of five cars for a scored day; the per-car set form, power cap, deadline rounding and energy definition; the arms, the population window and its conditioning; the model families, the full grid of settings searched, seeds and the selection rule; the base set U₀ (its h₀ printed) and the fallback offer with its numeric trigger, reference profile and amplitude range; the nine-profile delivery checks, the tariff, the solver tolerance, tie-breaking and the shortfall rule; the day score and its standardisation, the primary calibration rule with its step γ, starting level and window, and the secondary rule; the quantile levels of each arm and the trivial-bound rule; the target of 90% of days; the hypotheses with pass and fail thresholds (Section 9) and the single primary contrast; the uncertainty procedure (resampling whole weeks, since cars on the same day are not independent; number of draws stated); the reopening rule and the recovery window; the primary JPL track; the calendar and holiday source; which contrasts are confirmatory and which descriptive; the deviation rule (a change after the tag is reported beside the original result); the list of what has already been seen; and the author's written predictions of each outcome.

**Look-ahead prevention.** Current-session features use only events and app entries recorded before the decision time; every driver-linked feature uses only entries recorded before 10:00 (the final claimed flag and edit count are not used). Features from other sessions use only sessions that had ended before the decision time. Energy so far and energy after 10:00 come from the charging-current series cut at 10:00 and at 20:00. The replay engine remains the only code that reads unplug times. A typed plan is used only if its timestamp precedes 10:00. Refits see only sessions that ended before the refit date. Day-level calibration uses only past days, and each past day's score is the one stored on that day under the model then in force. The base set, cap, tariff, quantile grid, settings grid and calendar are committed in the tag and loaded, not recomputed. The scramble check is extended to days: altering every field that becomes known after a cutoff, including the later fields of sessions already plugged in at the cutoff, must leave every offer made before it identical, through the whole pipeline including the linear programme; the scramble replaces later values by draws from a different distribution, not a permutation, so that a path that uses counts alone is also caught. The tagged commit is the one that is run; a later change is reported as a deviation beside the original result.

**Possible second site.** The Norwegian residential dataset of Sørensen and others (2024; CC BY 4.0; twelve residential locations, 267 user identifiers; overnight charging, the setting of the 2024 paper's own example) has not been analysed by anyone involved in this project and is a candidate for a replication of the per-car layer on a never-seen site. It is optional and not part of the headline.

1. **Per-car layer.** Hours kept by a 90%-reliable lower bound on remaining stay, learned against population, by month 2018–2021. Expected: the learned bound keeps more hours before the change; both fall at the change; the gap afterwards is the result.
2. **Headline.** Offer size beside achieved delivery rate under the frozen rule targeting 90%, learned against population against oracle, by month; the oracle is the 100% line. If learned and population overlap, individual learning does not pay at fleet scale; if learned is clearly above at the same achieved rate, individual information survives aggregation.
3. **Offer size against share of days delivered**, one curve per arm, 2019 and 2021, with each arm's monthly delivery rate and its confidence interval from week-block resampling as a panel. The 2021 curve is expected inside the 2019 curve by about the drop in cars; the learned-to-oracle gap is the value of a perfect forecast; a small gap with a large reliability cost means the calibration, not the forecast, binds.
4. **Why fleet offers fail.** For each day, the share of present cars whose learned set was not inside their true set, against whether the offer was delivered under each check. Reported: how often the hindsight split succeeds despite failed bounds, how often the fixed allocation does, and whether failures concentrate on days when errors share a sign. Predictions, written after the earlier looks of Section 7: many days delivered under the hindsight split despite 20–40% of cars mispredicted; failures concentrated where errors share a sign. This shows how conservative the every-car-at-once condition is and when it stops being conservative enough.
5. **Theorem against data** is a line in the results table, not a figure: the day-level sample requirement of Theorem 4 at the two settings (312 and 1,121 days) and of Corollary 1 (2,379 and 6,089) beside the weekdays available (483 in development, about 250 in each rolling year, 182 in 2021).

## 9. Hypotheses (thresholds as written; the tag freezes them)

- H1. In the frozen development evaluation (September 2019 to February 2020), the learned offer (arm 4) is at least 10% larger than the population offer (arm 5) under the same calibration, with both arms' achieved delivery rates reported and a paired week-block interval that excludes zero. Confirmatory.
- H2. The union-bound offer (arm 3) is delivered on at least 98% of days and is at most half the size of arm 4. A check; expected to be nearly automatic.
- H3. From the reopening date defined in Section 7, adaptive day-level calibration restores a 90% running average of delivered days, over 20 weekdays, within 40 scored weekdays. A frozen offer calibrated only on pre-closure days is expected to be delivered on fewer than 80% of the first 20 scored weekdays; a count-based version of that was already seen (`docs/prior-looks.md`), so this clause is a check, not a blind test.
- H4. The per-car learned bound keeps at least 0.5 hours more than the population bound at matched 90% reliability on the frozen development evaluation sessions, with both achieved coverage rates reported. Confirmatory.

With 130 weekdays the standard error of a 90% rate is about 2.6 points; with 20 weekdays, about 6.7 points, so H3's first clause can only be read as a check. Monthly and per-site contrasts are descriptive. A hypothesis that fails is reported as it fails. Uncertainty comes from paired resampling of whole calendar weeks, with a two-week sensitivity check.

## 10. What could kill it

- Too few cars at 10:00 or too few 2021 weekdays to tell 90% of days from 80% (with 150 days the standard error of a 90% rate is about 2.4 points). Checkable on development data before anything else is built (Section 10a, D3); the 2021 count is reported in the frozen test and cannot choose the design.
- The per-session time series are unavailable or inconsistent for many sessions, so energy after 10:00 is not measurable and realised power limits are guesses; then the energy ceiling is approximate and the power cap is an assumption throughout, and the results say so (D1).
- The base set U₀ is so far from the sessions of a period that the structure-preserving programme returns α near zero even for the oracle; then the fallback offer applies, under the trigger of D9.
- Delivered energy mostly reflects the scheduler rather than the driver, so the energy bound is scored against a moving target; the typed-request sensitivity and D7 are the check.
- The learned bounds add nothing over the population bounds even before the change (a null result, still reported).
- Someone has done it: a published comparison of per-car and population offers scored by delivery in time order on real data would already answer the question. The closest works found as of November 2026 are listed in the README; two 2026 papers (Luo, Chen and Zhu; Park and others) could not be read in full and are unresolved.

## 10a. Checks on development data before the build commits

Each check runs on 25 April 2018 to 29 February 2020 only, with the rule fixed here; a failed check triggers the stated fallback, never a search of the sealed periods for a better setting. The numbers are pre-registered thresholds, not findings.

- **D1, energy after 10:00.** Fetch the time series for a fixed random sample of 300 development sessions (seeded). Proceed if at least 95% have a usable current series and the median absolute error of the integrated energy against the session's recorded kWh is at most 5%; then fetch the series for all sessions. Otherwise split the session total by the charging-done time, say the ceiling is approximate, and drop the realised-cap sensitivity.
- **D2, caps.** Count sessions by station; one cap per station group if more than 5% of development sessions are on stations whose measured 99th-percentile power exceeds 7.5 kW; otherwise 6.7 kW for all.
- **D3, cars with energy at 10:00.** Count, per weekday of the model-selection and frozen evaluation months, the present cars with more than 0.5 kWh remaining. Keep 10:00 if the median is at least 10 and the 10th percentile at least 4; otherwise test 09:00 by the same rule. Days with fewer than 5 such cars are not scored and are counted. If neither hour passes, the fleet layer is reported as not run and the per-car layer stands alone.
- **D4, unclaimed sessions.** If more than 30% of present cars are unclaimed with zero remaining energy, set their ceiling to zero by rule, report their share, and add the claimed-only analysis as a sensitivity.
- **D5, typed requests in time.** Keep the typed-request sensitivity only if at least 30% of present cars with energy remaining have a typed request recorded before 10:00 on at least 200 development weekdays; otherwise drop it and say so.
- **D6, driver history.** Report, per month, the share of present cars linked to a driver by 10:00 and with at least three earlier ended sessions; known and anonymous cars are evaluated separately.
- **D7, energy as a proxy for need.** Among sessions with a timely typed request, call delivered energy an adequate proxy if the median ratio of delivered to requested is within 0.8 to 1.2 and the rank correlation is at least 0.6; otherwise the headline says the energy is scheduler-shaped.
- **D8, calibration windows.** Use the split conformal rule only where the window holds at least 40 scored days; otherwise adaptive conformal inference alone, and the report says which rule ran on each day.
- **D9, the programme.** Switch to the one-dimensional fallback if the oracle's α is below 0.2 on more than 25% of scored weekdays of the model-selection months, or if the programme takes over 60 seconds on more than 5% of days; the chosen method is used for the whole sealed run.
- **D10, extreme quantiles.** Use a fitted lower quantile at a level only where its empirical miss rate on the model-selection months is within one percentage point of the level (half a point at the 0.005 level); elsewhere use the trivial bound and count those cars.
- **D11, censoring at refits.** Exclude from training any session that began within 24 hours of a refit date, in both population and learned arms.
- **D12, the base set.** Print h₀ and check that U₀ is non-empty with positive width in every step where the training fleet has capacity.

The shares of driver-linked sessions and of timely typed plans are recomputed here and stated with their definitions.

Before the `analysis-plan` tag, two things are reported from development data and the thresholds above may be reset once in their light, each reset logged with its reason in the change log: the counts behind D3 (cars present at 10:00 with energy remaining, per weekday of the model-selection and frozen evaluation months) and the week-to-week spread of offer sizes on the model-selection months, which decides whether H1's 10% is readable. After the tag, and after any sealed period is opened, no threshold changes.
